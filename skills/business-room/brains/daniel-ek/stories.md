# Stories: Daniel Ek

The stories he reuses, with the lesson he draws. One or two lines each.

1. **Growing up with piracy.** He saw that people wanted access to music, not ownership, and built a legal service that beat free piracy. Lesson: compete with the free habit by being easier. (S002)

2. **"Why would I rent my music?"** Early on, people pushed back on streaming; years later it looked obvious. Lesson: listen to doubters, but many are just not used to the idea yet. (S002)

3. **Lowering the price to zero.** Freemium let anyone try Spotify risk free and funneled them to paid. Lesson: in a crowded market, the entry price can be your sharpest weapon. (S001)

4. **2,000 partners.** Spotify went from about 250 device partners to more than 2,000, which brought in 28% of new sign-ups. Lesson: partners can be a distribution engine. (S001)

5. **The direct listing (2018).** Spotify listed without raising capital and without the usual IPO show. Lesson: the listing day matters less than the days after. (S005)

6. **Investing ahead of revenue, then cutting.** After the pandemic boom, costs grew twice as fast as revenue; he cut 6% of staff in January 2023 and 17% in December 2023. Lesson: do not let cheap capital set your cost base. (S004, S003)

7. **Podcasts in the same app.** Putting podcasts inside the music app met heavy resistance; it became a big growth lever. Lesson: one product with many formats can beat separate apps. (S002)

8. **100-hour weeks.** He used to work extreme hours and judge others for not doing the same, then changed how he managed time and energy. Lesson: the founder's energy is a resource to manage. (S006)
