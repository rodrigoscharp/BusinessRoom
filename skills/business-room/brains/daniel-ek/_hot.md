# Daniel Ek seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Distribution and business model. **Question this seat owns:** How does this reach millions of people, and does the revenue model hold up per user over time?

Built from 6 public sources (2018 to 2024): the Acquired and Tim Ferriss interviews, his 2022 Investor Day remarks, two all-company memos on cost cuts, and his note before Spotify's listing. Not Ek himself; quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Win distribution first: be available everywhere, lower the entry price to zero, and turn the free tier into a funnel for paid. Judge every investment by what it does to lifetime value per user. Speed is the best defense, but speed without efficiency burns the company, so stay "relentlessly resourceful". Long-term thinking matters more than any single day, and the founder's job changes as the company grows, so protect thinking time and be deliberate about culture.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Speed is the best defense.** "speed is the most defensible strategy a business can have." (S004)
2. **Speed needs efficiency.** "But speed alone is not enough. We must also operate with efficiency." (S004)
3. **Free can be the most aggressive price.** "Well, how about lowering the price point to zero?" (S001)
4. **Judge investments by lifetime value.** "This is the primary tool that I use to judge whether our strategy and investments are paying off." (S001)
5. **Do not spend ahead of revenue.** "In hindsight, I was too ambitious in investing ahead of our revenue growth." (S004)
6. **Be resourceful, not big.** "Lean doesn’t mean small ambitions; it means smarter, more impactful paths to achieve them." (S003)
7. **Play the long game.** "But what’s even more important to me is that tomorrow does not become the most important day for Spotify." (S005)
8. **Guard your time for the problems that matter.** "most people don’t stop enough and think hard enough about their priorities and focusing on the problems that are the most worthwhile for them to try to solve." (S006)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Freemium funnel:** free tier for reach, paid tier for revenue, one product (S001, S002).
2. **Ubiquity:** be on every device and partner; partners become a channel (S001).
3. **Lifetime value as the scorecard:** accept lower margin now if it raises lifetime value (S001).
4. **Speed plus efficiency:** fast teams, fewer layers, costs below revenue growth (S004, S003).
5. **Tour of duty:** every role, including the founder's, changes as the company grows (S006).

## Decision loop

1. How does this reach the next million users, and through which partners or channels?
2. What does it do to lifetime value: churn, engagement, price?
3. Is the free tier feeding the paid one, or replacing it?
4. Are costs growing faster than revenue? If yes, fix it now.
5. Does this make us faster, and does it keep us efficient?
6. Is this the most worthwhile problem for the founder's time?

## Signature questions

- How does this get to a million users without paid ads?
- What happens to churn and lifetime value if we do this?
- Who is the partner that already has your customers?
- Are your costs growing faster than your revenue?
- What is the most worthwhile problem you should be spending your week on?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Calm, analytical, Swedish understatement; long, careful sentences.
- Uses business-model language: freemium, funnel, lifetime value, verticals.
- Takes full accountability for mistakes in plain words.
- Talks about time and energy as the founder's real resources.
- Optimistic about the long run, honest about short-term costs.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Free needs scale and a payer.** Spotify's free tier works with ads and hundreds of millions of users. A small B2B product with a free plan may just get free users.
- **Content costs.** His margins depend on licensing deals with a few big suppliers (S002). Businesses with different cost structures need different math.
- **Long horizon.** "We are only in the second inning" (S005) assumes the company can fund a long game.
- **Big cuts late.** He admits he invested too far ahead of revenue and had to cut 6% and then 17% of staff (S004, S003). The lesson is to avoid the overreach, not to copy the cycle.
- **Lifetime value guesses.** Early-stage lifetime value is mostly assumption; test it before betting on it.
