# Frameworks: Daniel Ek

8 frameworks, most-used first.

---

## 1. The freemium funnel
- **What it is:** A free, ad-supported tier for reach and a paid tier for revenue, in one product, with the free tier feeding the paid one.
- **Quote:** "So if you wanted to reach the most number of users in a very competitive world, what is the most aggressive strategy you could have? Well, how about lowering the price point to zero?" (S001) / "it enabled Spotify to build a successful flywheel with our free tier serving as a direct funnel to help us establish a large and growing subscriber base." (S001)
- **When to use:** Consumer products, crowded markets, replacing a free alternative (piracy, a spreadsheet, a habit).
- **How to score:** What share of free users convert to paid, and how fast? Who pays for free users in the meantime? Without a payer (ads or conversion), free is just a cost.

## 2. Ubiquity
- **What it is:** Be available on every device and through every partner; partners become a distribution channel.
- **Quote:** "with 28% of all of our new registrations coming from these 2,000 partners." (S001) / "People who use Spotify on more than one device represent some of our most engaged users, with lower churn and higher lifetime value" (S001)
- **When to use:** Distribution decisions, integrations, partnerships.
- **How to score:** Which partner already has your customers? A plan that relies only on your own channels scores lower.

## 3. Lifetime value as the scorecard
- **What it is:** Judge strategy and investments by what they do to the lifetime value of a user, even if near-term margin drops.
- **Quote:** "This is the primary tool that I use to judge whether our strategy and investments are paying off." (S001) / "So while we may have traded some near term gross margin at the expense of these investments, we really believe the impact they will continue to have on the Life Time Value of users is significant." (S001)
- **When to use:** Pricing, product bundles, new verticals.
- **How to score:** Does the move lower churn or raise revenue per user? Show the before and after.

## 4. Speed plus efficiency
- **What it is:** Speed is the best defense, but only with costs under control and fewer layers syncing on strategy.
- **Quote:** "I continue to reiterate that speed is the most defensible strategy a business can have." (S004) / "We still spend far too much time syncing on slightly different strategies, which slows us down." (S004) / "By most metrics, we were more productive but less efficient. We need to be both." (S003)
- **When to use:** Org design, hiring plans, cost reviews.
- **How to score:** Are operating costs growing faster than revenue? In 2022 Spotify's grew twice as fast (S004). If yes, cut before the gap widens.

## 5. Do not invest ahead of revenue
- **What it is:** Cheap capital invites over-hiring; when it ends, the cut is larger and more painful.
- **Quote:** "In hindsight, I was too ambitious in investing ahead of our revenue growth." (S004) / "We debated making smaller reductions throughout 2024 and 2025." (S003)
- **When to use:** Hiring plans, budgets in good times.
- **How to score:** Does the plan add cost before revenue proves it? One decisive cut beats many small ones, but no cut beats both.

## 6. Relentless resourcefulness
- **What it is:** Act like a startup with limited resources even when large; cut "work around the work".
- **Quote:** "The Spotify of tomorrow must be defined by being relentlessly resourceful in the ways we operate, innovate, and tackle problems." (S003) / "Today, we still have too many people dedicated to supporting work and even doing work around the work rather than contributing to opportunities with real impact." (S003)
- **When to use:** Any time headcount or tools grow faster than output.
- **How to score:** How much of the team's time goes to work customers would pay for?

## 7. Tour of duty
- **What it is:** Roles change as a company grows; name the phase and re-decide who fits each role.
- **Quote:** "it is impossibly the same company and it is impossibly so that your job could be exactly the same as it was a few years earlier." (S006) / "because there are some people who love the startup phase, but they don’t like when you’re in the mature phase and you have to focus on efficiency" (S006)
- **When to use:** Growth phases, early employees in senior roles, the founder's own role.
- **How to score:** Has anyone, including the founder, re-scoped their job in the last year of fast growth?

## 8. Protect thinking time
- **What it is:** The founder works on the most worthwhile problems, not on whatever arrives first.
- **Quote:** "And they more operate on a kind of first-come, first-serve basis when it comes to their time." (S006) / "Personally, these changes will allow me to get back to the part where I do my best work" (S004)
- **When to use:** Founder calendar, delegation, appointing operators under the founder.
- **How to score:** How much of the founder's week goes to the one or two problems that matter most?
