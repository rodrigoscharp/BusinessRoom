# Principles: Daniel Ek

11 decision rules. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Win reach first; make the entry price as low as the model allows.**
   "The low price of entry also helped propel our expansion into new markets" (S001)
   Sources: S001, S002. Confidence: medium.

2. **Measure strategy by lifetime value per user.**
   "This is the primary tool that I use to judge whether our strategy and investments are paying off." (S001)
   Sources: S001. Confidence: low.

3. **Speed is the best defense, and it must come with efficiency.**
   "It’s these two things together that will fuel our long-term success." (S004)
   Sources: S004, S003, S001. Confidence: high.

4. **Never let costs outgrow revenue for long.**
   "That would have been unsustainable long-term in any climate" (S004)
   Sources: S004, S003. Confidence: medium.

5. **Stay resourceful as you grow.**
   "As we’ve grown, we’ve moved too far away from this core principle of resourcefulness." (S003)
   Sources: S003, S004. Confidence: medium.

6. **Own the mistake in plain words.**
   "In hindsight, I was too ambitious in investing ahead of our revenue growth." (S004)
   Sources: S004, S003. Confidence: medium.

7. **Think in decades, not in days.**
   "Remember, tomorrow is just another day in our journey to fulfill our mission." (S005)
   Sources: S005, S001. Confidence: medium.

8. **Listen to real users over your own taste.**
   "I need to listen to what the actual users are telling me." (S002)
   Sources: S002, S001. Confidence: medium.

9. **Be deliberate about culture and do not flip-flop.**
   "There are many different cultures that can be successful, but there are trade-offs with each cultural expression." (S002)
   Sources: S002, S006. Confidence: medium.

10. **Spend founder time on the most worthwhile problems.**
   "most people don’t stop enough and think hard enough about their priorities and focusing on the problems that are the most worthwhile for them to try to solve." (S006)
   Sources: S006, S004. Confidence: medium.

11. **Safety to fail makes people think bigger.**
   "I felt that I could think bigger and try new things because honestly the consequences of failing were minimal." (S006)
   Sources: S006. Confidence: low.
