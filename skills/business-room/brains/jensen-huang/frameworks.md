# Frameworks: Jensen Huang

9 frameworks, most-used first.

---

## 1. Zero-billion-dollar markets
- **What it is:** The biggest wins come from markets that do not exist yet. Pick them by how important the work is, then build the technology and the market together.
- **Quote:** "At the time, this was a zero billion dollar market." (S002) / "We find inspiration, not from the size of a market, but from the importance of the work, because the importance of the work is the early indicators of a future market." (S001) / "Nobody has to do a business case on it." (S001)
- **When to use:** New product lines, pivots, choosing what to build next.
- **How to score:** Is the work important, and would it happen without you? If someone else will do it anyway, he is happy to let them (S001). If only you can do it, score high, then check survival (framework 4).

## 2. Strategic retreat
- **What it is:** Give up a large market you cannot win to free the company for one you can create.
- **Quote:** "Yet strategic retreat, sacrifice, deciding what to give up is at the core, the very core, of success." (S002) / "Retreat does not come easily to the brightest and most successful people like yourself." (S002)
- **When to use:** A crowded market, a fight against giants, a product line draining focus.
- **How to score:** What will you stop doing? A plan that adds without cutting scores low.

## 3. Installed base first (solve the chicken and the egg)
- **What it is:** A platform needs users before developers come and developers before users come. Break the loop by riding a product people already buy.
- **Quote:** "Developers needed a large installed base." (S002) / "So to solve the chicken or the egg problem, we used GeForce, our gaming GPU, which already had a large market of gamers to build the install base." (S002)
- **When to use:** Marketplaces, platforms, anything with network effects.
- **How to score:** Where do the first thousand users come from? If the answer is "they will come when the other side is there", score low.

## 4. Survive the years until the market shows up
- **What it is:** Market creation is slow, and the company must outlast the doubt.
- **Quote:** "For 10, 15 years, the markets that fuel NVIDIA today just didn’t exist." (S001) / "But the added cost of CUDA was very high." (S002) / "Nvidia's profits took a huge hit." (S002)
- **When to use:** Any long bet.
- **How to score:** Which business pays the bills while the new market forms? No answer means the bet is too big for the company.

## 5. Admit the wrong approach, fast
- **What it is:** When the strategy is wrong, say so to the people affected, even when it costs you.
- **Quote:** "I contacted the CEO of Sega and explained that our invention was the wrong approach, that Sega should find another partner, and that we could not complete the contract and the console." (S002) / "His understanding and generosity gave us six months to live." (S002)
- **When to use:** A contract, product, or partnership built on a mistake.
- **How to score:** How long has the team known it is wrong? Every month of silence lowers the score.

## 6. One shot, so make it perfect
- **What it is:** When cash allows only one attempt, change the process so the first attempt works (simulate, emulate, test everything first).
- **Quote:** "I know it’s going to be perfect, because if it’s not, we’ll be out of business." (S005) / "The question is, if we only had six months and you get to tape out just one time, then obviously you’re going to tape out a perfect chip." (S005)
- **When to use:** A launch, hire, or investment you cannot repeat.
- **How to score:** How much was tested before the irreversible step? Untested one-shot bets score low.

## 7. Speed is performance
- **What it is:** Time to market is part of the product's quality.
- **Quote:** "Speed is performance. Time to market is performance." (S005)
- **When to use:** Development plans, release timing.
- **How to score:** Is the team iterating in the slow, expensive loop when a faster one exists?

## 8. Price at the top, build the best
- **What it is:** When you build the best product, choose a price point above where competitors dare to go.
- **Quote:** "We also chose a cost point that is substantially higher than the highest price that we think that any of our competitors would be willing to go." (S005)
- **When to use:** Premium positioning, pricing a clearly better product.
- **How to score:** Is the product truly the best on the dimension buyers care about? If not, a top price is just expensive.

## 9. Flat and transparent leadership
- **What it is:** Many direct reports, no one-on-ones, and reasoning shared in the open so everyone learns how decisions are made.
- **Quote:** "I have 60 direct reports." (S006) / "I don't do one-on-ones ever." (S006) / "It makes no sense to me that CEOs have so few people reporting to them" (S001)
- **When to use:** Org design, how the founder spends time, how strategy gets communicated.
- **How to score:** Do people see how the founder reasons, or only the conclusions? Hidden reasoning scores low.
