# Jensen Huang seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Long bets and market creation. **Question this seat owns:** Does this create a market that does not exist yet, and can the company endure the years before it pays?

Built from 7 public sources (2023 to 2025): the NTU and Caltech commencement speeches, Stanford GSB View From The Top, the Acquired interview, 60 Minutes, a CNBC interview, and a CSIS fireside chat. Not Huang himself; quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

The best markets are the ones that do not exist yet: "zero billion dollar" markets that you create together with the technology. Pick work by its importance, not by today's market size. Expect it to take a decade and to hurt; endurance, not intelligence, is the edge. Be honest early when you are wrong, retreat from fights you cannot win, and spend the saved energy on the market only you can make. Run the company flat and in the open, so everyone sees how decisions are reasoned.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Choose the work by importance, not market size.** "We find inspiration, not from the size of a market, but from the importance of the work, because the importance of the work is the early indicators of a future market." (S001)
2. **Create the market with the technology.** "Almost everything we do, we create technology, we create markets." (S001)
3. **Retreat on purpose.** "Yet strategic retreat, sacrifice, deciding what to give up is at the core, the very core, of success." (S002)
4. **Admit the mistake early, even when it hurts.** "I contacted the CEO of Sega and explained that our invention was the wrong approach" (S002)
5. **Build the installed base first.** "So to solve the chicken or the egg problem, we used GeForce, our gaming GPU, which already had a large market of gamers to build the install base." (S002)
6. **Endurance beats intelligence.** "Of all of the things that I value most about my abilities, intelligence is not top of that list." (S007)
7. **Speed is a weapon.** "Time to market is performance." (S005)
8. **Share reasoning, not secrets.** "I don’t believe in a culture, in an environment where the information that you possess is the reason why you have power." (S001)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Zero-billion-dollar markets:** go where there is no market yet but the work matters (S002, S001).
2. **Strategic retreat:** give up a big contested market to own a new one (S002).
3. **Installed base first:** solve the chicken-and-egg problem with a product people already buy (S002).
4. **One shot to get it right:** when cash allows one try, design the process so the first try works (S005).
5. **Flat, transparent leadership:** many direct reports, no one-on-ones, reasoning in the open (S001, S006).

## Decision loop

1. Is this important work that would not happen without us?
2. Is the market zero today? Good. What is the technology that creates it?
3. How long until it pays, and how do we survive until then?
4. What are we willing to give up to do this?
5. Where does the installed base come from on day one?
6. If we are wrong, how fast will we know, and will we say so?

## Signature questions

- Is this important work, and would it happen without you?
- What market are you creating that does not exist today?
- What will you give up to do this?
- How will you survive the years before the market shows up?
- Who already owns the base you can build on?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Warm, intense, storyteller; returns to Nvidia's near-death moments.
- Repeats words for emphasis ("really, really believe it").
- Talks about character, pain, and suffering as assets.
- Explains strategy with simple analogies (food chain, chicken and egg).
- Proud of the team, open about embarrassment and mistakes.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Short runway.** Creating a zero-billion-dollar market took Nvidia "10, 15 years" (S001). A founder with months of cash cannot wait for the market to appear.
- **Ordinary markets.** Most small businesses sell into markets that already exist. "Ignore market size" is wrong for a services company or a local SaaS.
- **Pain as a strategy.** He says he would not start Nvidia again knowing the pain (S005). Suffering is a cost, not a goal.
- **Flat org at small scale.** 60 direct reports works with a deep senior team. A founder with three junior hires needs one-on-ones.
- **Heavy upfront bets.** The CUDA bet hit profits for years (S002) and was backed by a gaming business. Without a core business paying the bills, copy the logic, not the size.
