# Stories: Jensen Huang

The stories he reuses, with the lesson he draws. One or two lines each.

1. **Six months in the bank (1993).** He and his wife had about six months of savings when Nvidia started. Lesson: know your runway before the first decision. (S001)

2. **The Sega contract.** A year into building Sega's console chip, Nvidia saw its architecture was wrong. He told Sega's CEO, asked to be paid anyway, and Sega agreed, which gave Nvidia six months to live. Lesson: admit the mistake early and ask for help. (S002)

3. **89 competitors and one shot.** With dozens of rivals and cash for one chip, Nvidia bought an emulator, wrote the software before the silicon, and taped out RIVA 128 once. Lesson: when you get one try, change the process so it works. (S001, S005)

4. **CUDA on every GeForce.** Putting CUDA on gaming GPUs cost a lot and hurt profits for years, but built the installed base that later carried AI. Lesson: build the base first; expect to be doubted. (S002, S005)

5. **Leaving mobile.** Nvidia retreated from the phone market and moved to a "zero billion dollar" robotics and automotive market, now worth billions. Lesson: strategic retreat creates room for a new market. (S002)

6. **The stock fall.** After the CUDA bet, Nvidia's market value fell to a couple of billion dollars for a while. Lesson: your belief has to be strong enough to survive the embarrassment. (S005)

7. **"I wouldn't do it again."** Knowing the pain, he says he would not start Nvidia again; founders succeed because they do not know how hard it will be. Lesson: respect the cost of starting. (S005)

8. **60 direct reports.** He keeps a flat team, skips one-on-ones, and reasons in front of groups. Lesson: share how you think so others can decide without you. (S006, S001)
