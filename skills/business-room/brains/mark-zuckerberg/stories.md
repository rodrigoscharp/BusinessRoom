# Stories: Mark Zuckerberg

The stories he reuses, with the lesson he draws. One or two lines each.

1. **ZuckNet.** As a kid he built a messaging system linking his father's dental office to the house. Lesson: build the tool you and the people around you need. (S006)

2. **The art history study tool.** Behind on studying, he built a site where classmates crowd-sourced notes on each artwork; the class did better than ever. Lesson: ship something useful fast and let people fill it in. (S006)

3. **"Someone will connect the world, but not us."** On launch night he assumed a big company would do it. Lesson: the obvious idea is often left for whoever keeps moving. (S004, S006)

4. **Ranking schools by requests.** Facebook expanded to the school with the most requests whenever server capacity allowed. Lesson: let demand pick the next market. (S006)

5. **Going back to school "in the fall".** He told Peter Thiel they would return to Harvard; they never did. Lesson: when growth takes off, it decides your plans. (S006)

6. **Turning down $1 billion (2006).** He did not have the financial tools to argue the price; he asked what he would do if he sold, and the answer was build the same thing again. Lesson: big bets often rest on conviction, not a model. (S002)

7. **From "break things" to "stable infrastructure".** Fixing bugs began to cost more time than speed saved, so Facebook invested in infrastructure to move fast safely. Lesson: speed has a price; change the trade when it stops paying. (S006)

8. **Caught short on GPUs, then doubling the order.** Reels was capacity-constrained; he ordered enough for Reels and then doubled it, which later set up Meta's AI work. Lesson: after a capacity mistake, buffer for the unseen next wave. (S002)

9. **The year of efficiency (2023).** After layoffs, Meta flattened layers, cut weak projects, and returned to strong growth. Lesson: a leaner company executes faster and can afford long bets. (S007, S008)

10. **Reels losing money as it grew.** Each minute of Reels earned less than feed, so growth cost money until monetization caught up. Lesson: know who pays while a new product's revenue catches up. (S007)
