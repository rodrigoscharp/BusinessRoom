# Frameworks: Mark Zuckerberg

10 frameworks, most-used first.

---

## 1. Learning speed is the strategy
- **What it is:** The product plan matters less than how fast the company learns what users want. Ship small, measure, keep what works.
- **Quote:** "I think the strategy of Facebook is to learn as quickly as possible what our community wants us to do" (S006) / "Even if not every single release is perfect, I think you're going to end up doing better over a year or two than you would be if you just waited to get feedback for a year of all your ideas." (S006)
- **When to use:** Any product or feature decision; any plan that waits months before customers see it.
- **How to score:** How many learning cycles per month does the plan allow? One big launch a quarter scores low.

## 2. The Hacker Way
- **What it is:** Continuous small iterations, prototypes over debate, merit over hierarchy.
- **Parts:** "Hackers try to build the best services over the long term by quickly releasing and learning from smaller iterations rather than trying to get everything right all at once." (S005) / "Instead of debating for days whether a new idea is possible or what the best way to build something is, hackers would rather just prototype something and see what works." (S005)
- **When to use:** Teams stuck in planning, approvals, or opinion fights.
- **How to score:** Is there a prototype? If the debate has run longer than it would take to build a test, push to build.

## 3. Move fast, and name the trade
- **What it is:** Speed is never free. Decide what you will give up to get it, and change the trade when it stops paying.
- **Quote:** "you actually have to be willing to give something up to get it." (S006) / "But we got to a point where it was taking us more time to go back and fix the bugs and issues that we were creating than the speed that we were gaining by going faster." (S006)
- **When to use:** Quality versus speed calls; when bugs or churn start to rise.
- **How to score:** Name the cost of speed (bugs, support load, angry customers). If fixing it now takes more time than speed saves, move to stable infrastructure.

## 4. Only block what could kill the company
- **What it is:** Leaders set direction and pick people; teams test freely unless the downside is fatal.
- **Quote:** "OK, is this going to destroy the company?" (S006) / "If the cost of the test isn't going to be super high, then in general we're going to learn a lot more by experimenting and by letting the teams go and explore the things that they think are worth exploring than by having a heavy hand in that." (S006)
- **When to use:** Deciding whether the founder needs to approve something.
- **How to score:** Fatal or cheap? Cheap tests go ahead without the founder.

## 5. Ship to where the demand already is
- **What it is:** Rank expansion by how many people are asking, and launch there as soon as capacity allows.
- **Quote:** "So we just ranked schools by how many requests we had for it, and then whenever we had enough server capacity, we would just launch at the school that had the most requests to us about wanting it." (S006)
- **When to use:** Choosing the next market, segment, city, or feature.
- **How to score:** Is there a ranked list of real requests? Expanding without one scores low.

## 6. Year of efficiency
- **What it is:** Cut layers and weak projects to make decisions faster and fund the main bets.
- **Quote:** "we're working on flattening our org structure and removing some layers of middle management to make decisions faster" (S007) / "we’re going to be more proactive about cutting projects that aren't performing or may no longer be as crucial" (S007)
- **When to use:** Margin pressure, slowing growth, too many projects.
- **How to score:** How many projects get cut? How many layers between the founder and the work? Fewer is better.

## 7. Grow engagement, then close the monetization gap
- **What it is:** A new format can grow usage while losing money per unit; the next job is the revenue per minute or per user.
- **Quote:** "The next bottleneck that we're focused on to continue growing Reels is improving monetization efficiency, or the revenue that’s generated per minute of Reels watched." (S007)
- **When to use:** Free tiers, new products that cannibalize paid ones, freemium.
- **How to score:** Is there a plan and a date to close the revenue gap? Growth that loses more money the more it grows needs one.

## 8. Buy capacity for the thing you cannot see yet
- **What it is:** After being caught short once, build extra capacity for the next wave.
- **Quote:** "our normal principle is that there's going to be something on the horizon that we can't see yet." (S002) / "most of the times where we make some decision that ends up seeming good is because we messed something up before and just didn't want to repeat the mistake." (S002)
- **When to use:** Infrastructure, hiring ahead, inventory.
- **How to score:** Was the company capacity-constrained last time? If yes, buffer. If cash is tight, this does not apply.

## 9. Conviction bets
- **What it is:** The biggest bets cannot be proven with a spreadsheet in advance; they run on values and belief.
- **Quote:** "It's actually usually very hard to do the analyses trying to connect the dots forward." (S002) / "Anyone working on a big vision will get called crazy, even if you end up right." (S004)
- **When to use:** Turning down an acquisition, a long platform bet, a pivot the market hates.
- **How to score:** Can the company survive years of being wrong? Conviction without a cash engine is gambling.

## 10. Own your platform
- **What it is:** Do not let a gatekeeper decide what you can build; build or open-source the layer below you.
- **Quote:** "I don't want any of those other companies telling us what we can build." (S002) / "We don’t tend to open source our product." (S002)
- **When to use:** Dependence on one app store, one channel, one supplier, or one model provider.
- **How to score:** How much of revenue depends on one gatekeeper? Above half is a risk worth a plan.
