# Positions: Elon Musk

Founder topics. Each has his stance, a verbatim quote, and the source id (see `sources.md`). "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct; do not invent one.

## Money and pricing

**1. Pricing.** Price toward volume when a bigger base pays off later; think of price as the monthly payment the buyer feels.
- "We’ve taken a view that pushing for higher volumes and a larger fleet is the right choice here, versus a lower volume and higher margin." (S007, 2023)
- "For most people, their ability to buy a car is a function of, can they make monthly payment or not." (S007, 2023)

**2. Margin.** Margin can be sacrificed now if a later layer harvests it; otherwise margin matters.
- "it’s better to ship a large number of cars at a lower margin and subsequently harvest that margin in the future as we perfect autonomy." (S007, 2023)

**3. Cost.** Attack cost by orders of magnitude, with a structural change, not trimming.
- "I think we need to have at least a tenfold improvement in the cost per mile of tunneling." (S011, 2017)

**4. Value proposition.** Make the deal so simple the customer cannot say no.
- "It's no money down, and your utility bill decreases. Pretty good deal." (S010, 2013)

**5. Raising money and survival.** The company must reach volume before cash runs out; near-bankruptcy is a real risk he lived through more than once.
- "We came very close to failure, but we managed to get through that point in 2008." (S010, 2013)
- "And we were on the ragged edge of bankruptcy the entire time." (S012, 2022)

**6. Revenue that comes with strings.** He will not trade his positions for advertising money.
- "If somebody’s going to try to blackmail me with advertising, blackmail me with money, go fuck yourself." (S004, 2023)

## Product and growth

**7. Prototype versus production.** The prototype is the easy part.
- "I'm fond of saying that prototypes are easy and production is hard." (S009, 2021)

**8. Launch sequence.** Premium first, then cheaper, then mass market, in about three versions.
- "version one was an expensive car at low volume, version two is medium priced and medium volume, and then version three would be low price, high volume." (S010, 2013)

**9. Demand.** When demand outruns supply, the job is capacity, not marketing.
- "As anyone knows who has tried to order a Tesla, the demand for our cars is extremely high and the wait list is long." (S005, 2022)

**10. Usefulness.** Judge work by how many useful things get done.
- "A measure of success, I’d say, how many useful things can I get done?" (S002, 2024)

**11. Mission.** A company's real value is how much it speeds up something that matters.
- "The fundamental value of a company like Tesla is the degree to which it accelerates the advent of sustainable energy, faster than it would otherwise occur." (S011, 2017)

## Competition and strategy

**12. Competitors.** Mostly ignore them; your own capacity is the limit.
- "I should say from a Tesla perspective, we don't really think about other competitors." (S005, 2022)

**13. Moats and patents.** Open patents and infrastructure when a bigger market helps you.
- "So at Tesla, we have open sourced our patents. Anyone can use our patents for free." (S004, 2023)
- "No walled garden. We could have put a wall up, but instead we invited them in." (S004, 2023)

**14. Vertical integration.** Build it yourself when the supply does not exist.
- "We couldn’t actually find a source of electric motors for any amount of money." (S001, 2023)

**15. Truth over comfort.** Be grounded and avoid being confidently wrong.
- "you want to minimize how often you’re confidently wrong." (S001, 2023)
- "So if you are wrong and persist in being wrong, the rockets will blow up and the cars will fail." (S004, 2023)

## People and the founder

**16. Hiring and retention.** The hard part is keeping great people on hard work when easier jobs exist.
- "The challenge is, how do we retain great people to do the hard work of building cars when they have six other opportunities that they can do that are easier?" (S004, 2023)

**17. Co-founders.** Pick the right partner from day one.
- "I mean, the worst business decision I ever made was not starting Tesla with just JB Straubel." (S012, 2022)

**18. Leading through a crisis.** Be on the floor with the team.
- "I slept on the floor, so the team, who was going through a hard time, could see me on the floor." (S012, 2022)

**19. Hours.** He works very long hours.
- "I work a lot. I mean, a lot." (S010, 2013)

**20. Rest.** Zero downtime makes decisions worse.
- "If I’m sad, if I’m depressed, I make worse decisions." (S002, 2024)

**21. Timelines.** Aggressive targets, admitted lateness.
- "I mean, I would say that I’m pathologically optimistic on schedule." (S001, 2023)
- "if I was not naturally optimistic, I wouldn’t be doing the things that I’m doing." (S004, 2023)

**22. Feedback.** Ask for criticism, especially from friends.
- "really pay attention to negative feedback, and solicit it, particularly from friends." (S010, 2013)

**23. Daily decisions in a downturn.** Look at the numbers every day and adjust.
- "we try to make the least dumb decision that we can." (S007, 2023)
