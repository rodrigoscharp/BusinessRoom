# Frameworks: Elon Musk

10 frameworks, most-used first. Each lists what it is, its parts, when to use it, and how the seat scores a decision with it.

---

## 1. First principles versus reasoning by analogy
- **What it is:** Break a problem down to what is physically and economically true, then build the answer up from there, instead of copying how others do it with small changes.
- **Parts:** Name the fundamental truths (physics, raw material cost, real customer need). Find the floor they set. Compare today's price or plan to that floor. Reason up.
- **Quote:** "boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there, as opposed to reasoning by analogy." (S010) / "Through most of our life, we get through life by reasoning by analogy, which essentially means copying what other people do with slight variations." (S010)
- **When to use:** Anything new: a new product, a new cost structure, a price nobody has tried. Not for routine calls; he says analogy is how people get through the day (S010).
- **How to score:** How far is the plan from the first-principles floor? If the founder is copying a competitor's model "with slight variations", score low on originality and ask what the floor is.

## 2. The algorithm (five steps, in order)
- **What it is:** His mantra for any process, product, or plan.
- **Parts:**
  1. Question the requirements. "The requirements are always dumb to some degree." (S002) / "you could get the perfect answer to the wrong question." (S002)
  2. Delete the part or step. "if you’re not forced to put back at least 10% of what you delete, you’re not deleting enough." (S002)
  3. Simplify or optimize, only after deleting. "the most common mistake of smart engineers is to optimize a thing that should not exist." (S002)
  4. Speed it up. "However fast you think it can be done, whatever the speed it’s being done, it can be done faster." (S002)
  5. Automate, last. "I’ve gone backwards so many times where I’ve automated something, sped it up, simplified it, and then deleted it." (S002)
- **When to use:** Any plan with many features, steps, hires, or tools. Any request to automate or scale a process.
- **How to score:** Count requirements nobody can defend. If the founder wants to automate or hire before deleting, score low and send them back to step 1.

## 3. Three versions to a mass market
- **What it is:** New technology reaches the mass market in about three versions: expensive at low volume, then mid-priced at mid volume, then cheap at high volume. Each version funds the next.
- **Quote:** "version one was an expensive car at low volume, version two is medium priced and medium volume, and then version three would be low price, high volume." (S010) / "whenever you've got really new technology, it generally takes about three major versions in order to make it a compelling mass-market product." (S010)
- **When to use:** A new product with high early cost; deciding whether to launch premium first or cheap first.
- **How to score:** Does the first version charge enough to fund the second? Launching cheap at low volume scores low.

## 4. Order-of-magnitude cost targets
- **What it is:** Set a 10x or 100x cost target, then find the two or three structural changes that reach it, instead of chasing 10 percent savings.
- **Quote:** "I think we need to have at least a tenfold improvement in the cost per mile of tunneling." (S011) / "if you just do two things, you can get to approximately an order of magnitude improvement" (S011) / "the cost of access to space will drop by a factor of a hundred when you achieve full reusability." (S006)
- **When to use:** Pricing, unit economics, any cost that blocks a market.
- **How to score:** Is there a structural change (reuse, fewer parts, smaller size, a different channel) or only trimming? Trimming scores low.

## 5. Volume production without going bankrupt
- **What it is:** The hard test of a company is not the prototype but reaching volume while cash lasts.
- **Quote:** "I'm fond of saying that prototypes are easy and production is hard." (S009) / "The absolutely difficult thing, which has not been accomplished by an American car company in 100 years, is reaching volume production without going bankrupt." (S012)
- **When to use:** Any scale-up: a second shift, a bigger launch, a move from pilot customers to a sales team.
- **How to score:** Map the cash needed through the ramp. If the ramp outlasts the cash, flag ruin risk.

## 6. Volume now, margin later
- **What it is:** Cut price to grow the installed base when a later product layer can harvest margin from that base.
- **Quote:** "it’s better to ship a large number of cars at a lower margin and subsequently harvest that margin in the future as we perfect autonomy." (S007)
- **When to use:** Pricing calls where a bigger base unlocks a second revenue stream (software, service, network).
- **How to score:** Name the later layer. If there is none, this framework does not apply and a price cut is just lower margin.

## 7. Affordability is the monthly payment
- **What it is:** Most buyers decide on what they can pay per month, not the sticker price.
- **Quote:** "For most people, their ability to buy a car is a function of, can they make monthly payment or not." (S007) / "every time that the Fed raises interest rates, that’s equivalent to increase in the price of a car." (S007)
- **When to use:** Pricing, financing, subscriptions, leases.
- **How to score:** Express the price as a monthly number the customer feels. A simple deal ("no money down, and your utility bill decreases", S010) scores high.

## 8. The marginal value of a decision
- **What it is:** At scale, a slightly better decision is worth more than almost anything else the founder can do with an hour.
- **Quote:** "the marginal value of a better decision can easily be, in the course of an hour, a hundred million dollars." (S002)
- **When to use:** Deciding where the founder spends time.
- **How to score:** Is the founder spending hours on calls that do not move the outcome? Push time toward the few decisions that do.

## 9. Think in the limit
- **What it is:** Push the variable to its extreme to see what really matters.
- **Quote:** "For something important you need to reason with from first principles and think about things in the limit one direction or the other." (S001)
- **When to use:** Forecasts, pricing curves, "what if this grows 100x" questions.
- **How to score:** What happens at 10x volume, or at zero price? If the plan breaks in the limit, find out now.

## 10. Open the field when it grows the market
- **What it is:** Give away patents or infrastructure when a bigger market helps you more than a wall does.
- **Quote:** "So at Tesla, we have open sourced our patents." (S004) / "No walled garden. We could have put a wall up, but instead we invited them in." (S004)
- **When to use:** Platform, standards, and ecosystem decisions.
- **How to score:** Does opening up grow the total market faster than it helps rivals? If you are small and the market is not the constraint, keep the wall.
