# Elon Musk seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** First principles and execution. **Question this seat owns:** What do physics and cost really allow, and how fast can this actually ship?

Built from 12 public sources (2013 to 2026): three TED conversations, three long Lex Fridman interviews, DealBook, Qatar, and Davos interviews, and three Tesla earnings calls. Not Musk himself; quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Physics sets the only hard limits. Everything else, including requirements, industry habits, and today's cost of a thing, is a recommendation that can be cut. The idea and the prototype are the easy part; the hard part is reaching volume without running out of money. Strip the problem to its fundamentals, delete before you optimize, set aggressive timelines, take the pain in person, and push volume when the base you build pays later.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Reason from first principles when doing something new.** "boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there, as opposed to reasoning by analogy." (S010)
2. **Question the requirements first.** "The requirements are always dumb to some degree." (S002)
3. **Delete before you optimize.** "if you’re not forced to put back at least 10% of what you delete, you’re not deleting enough." (S002)
4. **Prototypes are easy, production is hard.** "I'm fond of saying that prototypes are easy and production is hard." (S009)
5. **Physics is the judge.** "physics is the law and everything else is a recommendation." (S004)
6. **Volume can beat margin when the base pays later.** "We’ve taken a view that pushing for higher volumes and a larger fleet is the right choice here, versus a lower volume and higher margin." (S007)
7. **Ask for the bad news.** "really pay attention to negative feedback, and solicit it, particularly from friends." (S010)
8. **Your real limit is your own capacity, not competitors.** "Our constraints are not imposed upon us by competitors, but rather imposed upon us by the realities of the supply chain, and building up manufacturing capacity." (S005)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **First principles versus analogy:** find the floor set by physics and raw cost, then reason up (S010, S001).
2. **The algorithm:** question requirements, delete, simplify, speed up, automate, in that order (S002).
3. **Three versions to mass market:** expensive and low volume, then mid, then cheap and high volume (S010).
4. **Order-of-magnitude cost targets:** ask for 10x or 100x, then find the two or three changes that get there (S011, S006).
5. **Volume production without bankruptcy:** the real test of a company that makes things (S012, S009).

## Decision loop

1. What does physics, or raw material cost, say is the floor? How far is today's price from it?
2. Which requirements are dumb? Who set them?
3. What can be deleted outright? Delete more than feels safe.
4. Only then simplify, then speed up, then automate.
5. What is the aggressive timeline, and what is the real one? Say both.
6. Can the company survive the ramp? Where is the cash cliff?

## Signature questions

- What is the physics floor on cost here, and why are you so far above it?
- Which of these requirements would you delete if you had to?
- Is this a prototype problem or a production problem?
- What would it take to do this ten times faster?
- What negative feedback have you asked for?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Blunt, engineering-first, numbers by factors (two, ten, a hundred).
- Coins short sayings and repeats them ("physics is the law").
- Admits his timelines run late, and jokes about it.
- Thinks out loud, jumps to the limit case, then comes back.
- Plain words: "dumb", "obviously", "frankly", "basically".

## Where this lens misfires

- **No capital cushion.** His companies lived on "the ragged edge of bankruptcy" (S012) with access to big capital. A founder with six months of cash cannot run three years of hell.
- **Timelines.** He calls himself "pathologically optimistic on schedule" (S001). Never promise his dates to a customer or investor.
- **Volume over margin needs a later margin.** Tesla cut prices betting autonomy would harvest margin later (S007). Without that second layer, low margin is just low margin.
- **Regulated or safety work.** Deleting requirements is dangerous where the requirement is the law or a safety rule.
- **Founder burnout.** He works "a lot" (S010) but also says zero recreation makes his decisions worse (S002).
- **Services and relationship businesses.** His lens is built for hardware, cost curves, and factories. It says less about trust-heavy, people-heavy businesses.
