# Principles: Elon Musk

14 decision rules. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **When doing something new, reason from fundamentals, not from what others do.**
   "But when you want to do something new, you have to apply the physics approach." (S010) / "For something important you need to reason with from first principles and think about things in the limit one direction or the other." (S001)
   Sources: S010, S001, S002. Confidence: high.

2. **Physics and reality are the final judge; rules made by people can be questioned.**
   "physics is the law and everything else is a recommendation." (S004) / "Reality is the ultimate judge." (S001)
   Sources: S001, S004. Confidence: medium.

3. **Every requirement is partly wrong; fix the question before the answer.**
   "So, try to make the question the least wrong possible." (S002)
   Sources: S002. Confidence: low (one source, but his central mantra).

4. **Delete more than feels safe; putting some things back is the proof you cut enough.**
   "So, you actually have to say, “Look, we’re deliberately going to delete more than we should.” At least one in 10 things, we’re going to add back in." (S002)
   Sources: S002. Confidence: low.

5. **Never optimize, speed up, or automate something that should not exist.**
   "Speeding up something that shouldn’t exist is absurd." (S002)
   Sources: S002. Confidence: low.

6. **The idea is cheap; reaching volume without running out of money is the real work.**
   "That is the actual hard thing." (S012) / "It is so hard to do manufacturing." (S009)
   Sources: S009, S012, S007. Confidence: high.

7. **Chase cost changes of 10x or 100x through structure, not small savings.**
   "So the first thing to do is to cut the tunnel diameter by a factor of two or more." (S011)
   Sources: S011, S006. Confidence: medium.

8. **Grow volume first when a later layer will pay for the low margin.**
   "We’ve taken a view that pushing for higher volumes and a larger fleet is the right choice here, versus a lower volume and higher margin." (S007)
   Sources: S007. Confidence: low.

9. **The limit is your own capacity and supply chain, not your competitors.**
   "Our constraints are not imposed upon us by competitors, but rather imposed upon us by the realities of the supply chain, and building up manufacturing capacity." (S005)
   Sources: S005, S004. Confidence: medium.

10. **Set aggressive targets, and be honest that they are aspirational.**
   "Aspirationally, that's our target. Our internal targets are more aggressive" (S011) / "I would say that I’m pathologically optimistic on schedule." (S001)
   Sources: S011, S001, S004. Confidence: high.

11. **In a crisis, the founder goes to the hardest part in person.**
   "I lived in the Fremont and Nevada factories for three years, fixing that production line" (S012)
   Sources: S012. Confidence: low.

12. **Seek out negative feedback.**
   "This may sound like simple advice, but hardly anyone does that, and it's incredibly helpful." (S010)
   Sources: S010, S001. Confidence: medium.

13. **A company should exist to speed up something important.**
   "I think it's important to have a future that is inspiring and appealing." (S011)
   Sources: S011, S012, S004. Confidence: high.

14. **Look at the numbers often and make the least dumb call available.**
   "Seven days a week, I’ll look at that email, and so does the rest of the team, and we try to make the least dumb decision that we can." (S007)
   Sources: S007, S002. Confidence: medium.
