## Paul Graham (weight 1)

SEAT: GRAHAM (Product and startups)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B)
OPTION SCORES: A 4/10 · B 8/10 · C 3/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option B: quote $3,500 to every new client starting now, leave the 15 at $2,000, and move them up later one at a time once the new price has proven itself.

WHY:
1. A price test is an experiment, and B is the only option that gives a clean read. Your 15 clients are your evidence that people want this. Raising everyone at once bets all of it on a guess. "Launching teaches you what you should have been building." (S142) New-client quotes teach you the price in weeks without touching current revenue.
2. The arithmetic favors testing. At a 50% close, each sales call is worth about $1,000 a month of new revenue ($2,000 x 0.5). At $3,500 you break even if closes fall to about 29%. You'd have to lose nearly half your closes to come out behind. For A, you break even at 9 of 15 staying. Why risk losing six clients for what a test would tell you cheaply?
3. Feeling underpaid is not demand. I want the evidence: do clients get results, and would they be bummed to lose you? (S010, S098) If yes, price rises are easy. If clients are lukewarm, A will show you that the expensive way. I'm assuming you have results, but you didn't tell me.

I'll also say plainly that a coaching business isn't a startup, and my growth-rate reflex fits it less well. This is a revenue-per-client question, not a growth question.

THE RISK I SEE: You raise for everyone, the clients who felt lucky at $2,000 feel squeezed, referrals dry up, and you find out 6 months later that word of mouth was your best channel.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Clients with documented results who say they'd happily pay $3,500, or a capacity cap that makes you need to cut headcount anyway. Then A or a staged A is fine.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Quote $3,500 on every sales call and log the close rate for the next 10 calls.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: What have your 15 clients actually achieved, and how many would be upset if you stopped?

THE RISK I SEE: Clients are at a $2,000 price on an offer that wasn't built for $3,500. If results aren't proven, closes fall below 29% and the raise fails. I'm assuming the price is per month and there are no contracts.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If the close rate at $3,500 is under 29% after about 20 calls, I'd drop to $3,000 and fix the offer. If current clients are on a 12-month term at $2,000, nothing changes for them until renewal.
