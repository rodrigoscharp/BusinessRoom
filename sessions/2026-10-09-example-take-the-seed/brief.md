# Brief: raise a seed round or keep bootstrapping

Mode: full council, all seven seats, three rounds. An example decision for a bootstrapped SaaS founder.

## THE BRIEF

**Decision:** A bootstrapped B2B SaaS founder has a term sheet and must answer within three weeks. Options: (A) take the $4M seed at a $20M post-money valuation from a well-known VC (20% dilution, one board seat, expectation of 3x revenue in 18 months); (B) raise a smaller $1M from five operator angels at a $12M cap on a SAFE (about 8% dilution, no board seat); (C) keep bootstrapping and turn the term sheet down.

**The business:** Scheduling and dispatch software for home-services companies (plumbers, HVAC, electricians), about 200 customers paying $200/month on average. MRR $40k, growing 4% a month for the last 12 months. Gross margin 82%. Monthly logo churn 2.5%. Profitable at a small level ($6k/month net). Customers come mostly from word of mouth and one Facebook group the founder runs; no paid acquisition, no outbound sales team.

**Team:** two co-founders (one technical, one sales and customer success), four employees (two engineers, one support, one implementation). The CEO founder works about 65 hours a week and does all sales calls and most onboarding.

**Resources:** $180k in the bank. No debt.

**What success looks like:** a category-leading company in home-services software within five years; the founders say they would be happy with a $50M+ outcome and do not need a billion-dollar one. Horizon: 5 years.

**Founder's lean:** the CEO wants to take option A ("this is our shot, and competitors with funding will outspend us"). The technical co-founder prefers C.

**Context (assumed):** two venture-backed competitors exist, each said to be above $3M ARR; neither focuses on companies under 10 technicians, which is this founder's core market.
