# Round 2: debate

## Graham (weight 3)

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: HORMOZI. He says prepay replaces the round and 2.5% churn is fine. On prepay: most of the $120k is revenue those 60 customers would pay anyway, pulled forward at a two-month discount. It's a loan from customers that covers about one hire for one year, and next year's cash from those 60 is already spent. Do it, but it doesn't buy the seats. On churn: 0.975^12 is 0.74, so a quarter of customers leave every year. Of about 13 gross adds a month, churn eats 5. Cut churn to 1.5% and net growth goes from 4% to 5% a month with no new spend. Over five years that's 18.7x instead of 10.5x. "Under 3%" may be a fine SMB benchmark. Growth is the compass (Startup = Growth, S100), and churn is the cheapest lever on it.
I CONCEDE: Hormozi is right that nobody has measured CAC, so the money has no proven place to go yet. Because of that, the prepay offer and the churn calls happen before signing, not after.
ON THE CRUXES:
1. They don't need it, and that's the best reason to take a little. "if you want to raise money, the best thing you can do is get yourself to the point where you don't need to." (S093)
2. It's a leak, and fixing it is worth a full point of monthly growth.
3. An onboarding owner first. I suspect the early churn starts at onboarding. Then a closer.
4. A split founding team is reason enough to refuse A. B is the option both can sign without a board deciding for them. The deadline belongs to the VC. The angels can close later.
STANCE: Conditional yes on B.

## Buffett (weight 3)

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: MARTELL. His argument is that C can't carry the ladder, because "You should not be capital constraint." The arithmetic says otherwise. Take $6k a month of profit, subtract a closer at about $10k a month all-in (my assumption), and you get a $4k monthly burn that $180k covers for 45 months. The $1,500 assistant fits too. The constraint is the CEO's calendar, and money doesn't fix that. Selling 8% to fix a calendar fails the issuance test: "You can’t get rich trading a hundred-dollar bill for eight tens." (Stock issuance test, S087)
I CONCEDE: Hormozi is right that the prepay is the cheaper capital, and by a mile. Sixty customers at $2,000 brings in $120k. At 2.5% churn a monthly customer pays about $2,100 in year one, so the real discount is about $6k. That's a nickel per dollar, and it locks in a year of retention. B's $1M costs 8% of the $50M the founders want, which is $4M, or about $4 per dollar at their target. That makes my round 1 scoring a mistake: I ranked B over C without running C's runway with a closer. Two fixes. This is float, not profit, so it runs down over the year to an average of about $60k. And the $500 setup fee should apply to new customers only, because charging it to people who are already set up fails the newspaper test (S032).
ON THE CRUXES:
1. They don't need the money to survive. Prepay plus profit pays for the closer. Take B only if the prepay comes up short.
2. Churn is a leak. Cutting it from 2.5% to 1.5% stretches a customer's life from 40 to 67 months, and lifetime gross profit from $6,560 to about $10,900.
3. A closer first, paid from profit. Onboarding second, because that's where churn starts.
4. Don't take a one-way door with a split partnership, so A is out. The three-week clock is Mr. Market's quote and says nothing about the business (Mr. Market, S060).
The best banker a farm ever had is a customer who pays in advance.
STANCE: Conditional yes on C plus annual prepay, with B as the fallback.
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10 · B 6/10 · C 7/10 · PILLAR SCORE: 7/10 · CONFIDENCE: 3/5 · RUIN RISK: none
MOVED: yes, from B to C. By my own tests the prepay is cheaper capital, and C pays for a closer out of profit.
MY CONDITIONS FOR YES:
1. At least 40 customers sign annual prepay by day 14. If fewer sign, take B.
2. A closer is hired within 60 days, and the CEO is under 50 hours a week by day 90.
3. With the closer on payroll, cash stays at or above about $210k, which is 12 months of burn if revenue falls by a third (Financial staying power, S095).

Resolved:

Still open (the real crux): **is prepay capital?** The factual question splits on how many hires C must carry. With two hires (onboarding owner plus closer, about $25k a month), Bezos, Hormozi, and Wickman show C burns $19k to $31k a month and lasts about 9 to 10 months even with prepay. Five seats put the onboarding owner first, so the two-hire case is the plan on the table. Buffett and Martell's position survives as a decision rule: if prepay lands strongly, decline both raises.
