## Minority report

Graham: "the average leads are where you learn why growth is 5%. Those are the people who didn't buy." For this to be right: Growth is capped by an unsolved demand or offer problem, and no qualified leads are going unanswered, so handing calls to a closer only adds noise.

## Conditions

1. Record every sales call for 30 days before the closer takes any live lead.
2. Write down the top five objections and the top lost-deal reasons from those recordings.
3. Write a sales script good enough that a new person can reach 70 to 80% from the documents alone.
4. Pay the closer on cash collected only, with a clawback for customers who churn within 90 days.
5. The founder keeps all discovery calls, pricing calls, and top leads. The closer gets average qualified leads only.
6. The founder reviews the closer's recorded calls every week.
7. Count qualified leads that went unanswered or were delayed, and check revenue retention, before the closer starts.
8. No full-time AE at $120k base in this period.

## What to do next

- **This week:** Start recording every sales call. Pull the last 60 days of leads and count any qualified ones that went unanswered or waited too long. Check churn and net revenue retention.
- **Weeks 1 to 4:** Log objections and lost-deal reasons after every call. Draft the script and a one-page definition of a qualified lead.
- **Week 3:** Post the commission-only closer role with cash-collected pay and a 90-day clawback. Screen for people who have sold at your deal size.
- **Week 5:** Closer starts on average qualified leads only. The founder keeps discovery, pricing, and top leads.
- **Weekly from week 5:** Review the closer's recorded calls and compare close rate, deal size, and churn against the founder's numbers.
- **After the closer's 20th call:** Run the kill check. Then decide whether to expand the closer's lead share.

**Cheapest test:** Count the qualified leads from the last 60 days that went unanswered or were delayed, and record the next 10 calls. It costs nothing. If leads are being dropped, it is a capacity problem and a closer helps. If not, the founder should spend the freed hours on leads and the offer.
**Kill criteria:** Stop the closer if their close rate is under half the founder's after 20 calls, if their customers churn within 90 days at a higher rate than the founder's, or if no capable closer accepts the role because deal size is too small. If MRR growth drops below 5% a month for two straight months during the trial, pull the leads back and revisit.
**Revisit:** After the closer's 20th call or 90 days, whichever comes first. Reopen the full-time AE question only when the playbook is proven, qualified leads are clearly going unanswered, and MRR can carry a $10k a month hire without risking runway.
